How the model works

The country

Youtopia is fictional. Its population is 800,000, with 400,000 households. Four household profiles each represent 100,000 similar households. All country figures are teaching assumptions, not forecasts for Australia.

Production, demand and prices

Households spend 80% of income remaining after income tax, the fictional extra-tax or rebate adjustment and project contributions. Other demand is held fixed within each round. Government purchases and private project purchases are added to household spending.

Real output is limited by capacity. While capacity remains available, the price index stays at 100. Beyond that limit, extra nominal demand raises the model price level. This simple short-run model has sticky prices and a hard capacity limit. It does not model continuing inflation, interest rates, international spillovers or every feedback effect.

Demand = household spending + other demand + government purchases + private project purchases
Real output = minimum of demand and capacity
Price index = 100 × maximum of 1 and demand ÷ capacity

Worker equivalents are estimated at $100,000 of real output per worker. This is a fixed-productivity assumption, not a census of employed people or an unemployment rate.

Pay rises and tax

Tax bands are the Australian adult resident 2026–27 bands, with the full tax-free threshold. Medicare levy, offsets, deductions, benefit withdrawals and student tax rules are excluded. Youtopia’s optional equal-dollar extra tax or rebate is a separate, fictional fiscal-policy choice. It changes each household’s tax bill by the same dollar amount and does not alter the Australian marginal tax bands.

A pay rise reallocates business earnings into a household group’s wages. Spending from retained business earnings and other demand are held fixed. The rise is not simply added to GDP: only the resulting change in spending enters the demand model. Nominal business earnings are the residual of output value minus wages.

Public goods

The four-player experiment starts each participant with 20 payoff tokens. Each contributed token creates 0.4 benefit points for each participant. A participant’s payoff is tokens kept plus shared benefit. The 1.6 group multiplier is an experimental payoff rule, not a return on real infrastructure or a GDP multiplier.

For the country connection, each token costs $100 per household in its 100,000-household group: $10m nationally. Contributions are spent once on the project. Voluntary funding appears as private project purchases; an equal levy appears as earmarked government revenue and project purchases. Identical contribution amounts have identical effects on model demand. Benefit points stay separate from money and GDP.

Solo mode uses stated, fictional contributions from three model households. Pass-and-play lets four students make private choices on one device. It does not connect different devices.

Budget and rounds

Budget balance equals revenue minus spending. Editing a policy changes the current round’s projected balance; it does not spend another year’s budget. Closing a round adds its balance to the debt calculation once. Debt projection shows the expected position at the end of the current round.

Round 2 adds $2.25bn of other demand. Round 3 retains that demand but reduces capacity by 10%. Earlier decisions remain in place.
