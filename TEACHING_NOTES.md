## Guided flow

1. Learn: explain the concept in short connected prose, with term help and the named lesson connection.
2. Predict: compare a controlled example and discuss a prediction. Selecting an answer never applies a decision. Feedback explains the mechanism; a wrong prediction does not block learning.
3. Explain: apply once, show before/after figures and reveal one causal step at a time. Then discuss a trade-off and reveal an example explanation when ready.

Explore retains the original sliders, the tax calculation and the four-player public-goods activity. It adds optional live explanations and a debrief after applying a decision. Existing saved economies are retained. Preferences and guided progress use a separate browser-local key; no student information or response collection is introduced.

## Accuracy boundaries

- The budget balance is revenue minus spending. Neither a deficit nor a surplus proves a policy is beneficial.
- The price index is a level relative to starting prices, not a measured annual inflation rate.
- Extra spending can raise real output only while productive capacity is available. The model does not make new infrastructure expand capacity immediately.
- The fictional equal-dollar income-tax adjustment is distinct from the Australian progressive tax bands and from the project levy. Negative internal `rebate` values represent extra tax. Both household income and government revenue include the adjustment with opposite signs.
- The tax example uses adult resident 2026–27 marginal rates, excluding Medicare levy and offsets. The $1,000 rise from $45,000 adds $300 income tax and leaves $700 before other adjustments. Rates do not change when a household gets a pay rise.
- A public good is non-rival and non-excludable; a government-funded service is not automatically a pure public good. Payoff points are separate from national project spending.
- Public project purchases are counted once. With equal contribution vectors, compulsory and voluntary funding have the same macro demand effect; the levy adds matching government revenue and spending.
- National figures remain a simplified short-run classroom model. Other demand and spending from business earnings are held fixed within a round; household spending is 80% of retained income. The wages, output, debt and capacity assumptions remain explicit in Model notes.
